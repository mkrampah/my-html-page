    1. Problem

    Launching a data bundle or a mobile-money feature is expensive to get wrong, and live tests are slow. Can a simulated crowd of customer agents give an early, cheap read on which offer works for which segment, before anything goes live?

    2. Method

    Six assumed personas generate the synthetic customers. Each customer draws a budget and a weekly data need around the persona’s average, then weighs how well the offer covers that need against what it costs and how much data would go to waste. The customer buys, hesitates (would ask a question) or ignores, and records the main reason. Offers A and B are tested on the same customers, so differences come from the offer and not from luck. Everything runs in the browser, and the same seed always gives the same result.

    4. Limits

    • Assumed, not measured. The persona numbers are invented, so the output shows how the model behaves and not how real customers behave.
    • Not validated. Nothing has been compared with real usage or survey data yet. This is the most important next step.
    • Rules, not LLM agents. Decisions and reasons come from a formula. LLM-driven agents are the planned upgrade.
    • Simplified world. No network coverage, competitors, social influence, repeat purchases or sales channels.
    • Coarse personas. Six segments can hide real differences and can encode stereotypes.
    • One run per seed. No confidence intervals yet, so small gaps between offers should not be trusted.

    # MTN Crowd Mind: customer simulation prototype

Use Case #52, Team-021-Ventura. Independent prototype by Michael Krampah.

Simulates a crowd of synthetic customers reacting to two product offers, so a product can be tested before launch.
Synthetic data only. No MTN data and no real customer information.

## Run it
Open index.html in any browser. No install, no server, no API keys.

## What it does
1. Generates N customers from 6 assumed personas (student, market trader, rideshare driver, small-business owner, remote professional, rural farmer).
2. Each customer decides: buys, hesitates or ignores, and gives a reason.
3. Compares Offer A and Offer B on take-up, revenue per customer contacted and objections, overall and by segment.
4. The same seed gives the same customers for both offers (a paired test) and reproducible results.

## How a decision is made
utility = 2 x fit - price pressure - wasted-data penalty + noise
P(buy) = logistic(4 x (utility - 0.6))
fit = share of weekly data need covered.
price pressure = sensitivity x weekly price / weekly budget.

## Assumptions
Persona shares, budgets, data needs and price sensitivities are illustrative. Edit the SEG table in index.html to change them.
